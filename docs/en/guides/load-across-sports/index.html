Load across sports: cycling, running, swimming and strength on one scale

Your body does not distinguish between cycling and running when it comes to fatigue. For fitness, fatigue and form to be right, every session needs a load number on the same scale.

Updated: October 6, 2026 · 5 min read

One scale for everything

The reference is always the same rule: one hour exactly at your threshold equals 100 points. In cycling the threshold is FTP, in running threshold pace, in swimming critical swim speed (CSS). That way a ride and a run can be added up, and fitness, fatigue and form apply across all sports.

How precise it is per sport

  • Cycling with a power meter: most precise (TSS).
  • Running with pace: good (rTSS). Even better with running power in watts; somewhat too low on hills without elevation data.
  • Swimming with distance and time: usable (sTSS). Because water resistance rises steeply with speed, load increases more with intensity than in running.
  • Heart rate only: estimate via your threshold heart rate. Good for steady sessions, less precise for intervals.
  • Strength and core: estimate from duration and perceived effort (0 to 10). There is no established formula for it, so ZonePit marks it as an estimate.

Every session shows what its load is based on, so you know how far to trust the number.

What it means for planning

  • No two hard days in a row, not even across sports. A hard run after hard bike intervals is just as tiring.
  • Your main sport gets the key sessions. Other sports complement at an easy level.
  • Running needs more caution when building up than cycling or swimming, because impact stresses tendons and bones more. Volume therefore grows more slowly.
  • Leg-heavy strength training does not belong right before a key session.
